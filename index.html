The practical half

TorontoTTC subway & streetcar, UberKids under 12 ride the TTC free. On weekends and holidays a single Day Pass covers two adults plus up to four kids — worth it for 26–27 Dec. Load PRESTO, or just tap a credit card at the gate.
AngusRental car (yours)Ask the desk to confirm winter tires, not all-seasons — it is not automatic in Ontario. Nine people means two vehicles or one large van. Everything worth doing is 20–70 min away, and the house is your base.
MontréalSTM métro, UberChildren under 11 travel free with a paying adult. Buy an OPUS card at any station. The métro is warm and frequent and connects to the Underground City, which matters at −18 °C.
QuébecWalk, taxi, Uber — no métroQuébec City has no subway. Old Québec is small but steep and icy; the funicular between Upper and Lower Town is a few dollars and worth it every single time with the stroller and the grandparents.

Book before you fly

  • Christmas Day, 25 DecRipley's Aquarium timed tickets, and any restaurant at all. Nearly everything else in Toronto is shut.
  • New Year's Eve in BarrieRestaurants are fully reserved by early December. Decide now: book, or plan to cook.
  • Tubing sessionsSnow Valley, Horseshoe Valley and Valcartier all sell timed slots that fill over the holidays.
  • VIA RailMontréal → Québec on 5 Jan, and the return on the 8th. Économie fares climb as the date nears, and nine seats together need lead time.
  • Scandinave SpaBlue Mountain, 19+ only. Sells out on holiday weekends.
  • Any sit-down dinnerA table of nine needs a phone call, not a walk-in.

The car seat problem

  • Sort this before you landA one-year-old legally needs a proper child seat in a private vehicle in both Ontario and Québec, and Uber drivers do not carry one.
  • Bring a travel seatA lightweight convertible seat checks free on most airlines and solves Toronto, Montréal and Québec in one move.
  • Licensed taxisare treated differently from ride-hail under both provinces' rules — but confirm with the operator rather than assuming.
  • The rental carReserve a child seat with it for the Angus week, or use your own.
  • Nine peopleis always two Ubers. Budget roughly double every fare you see quoted.

Coming from 30 °C

  • Layers, never cottonThermal base, fleece or wool mid, windproof outer. Cotton holds sweat and then freezes you.
  • Boots rated to −30with real grip. The one thing not to cheap out on, for all nine.
  • Slip-on ice cleatsAbout $20 a pair. For the two over-60s and whoever is carrying the baby, they change the trip.
  • The babyOne layer more than an adult, a covered stroller or a carrier under your own coat, mittens on elastic. Never a puffy coat inside the car seat harness — blanket over the top instead.
  • Mittens beat glovesfor the nine-year-old, every time.
  • Sunglasses and lip balmSnow glare is real and the indoor air is desert-dry.
  • Hand and toe warmersBuy a box at any Canadian Tire on day one. Cheap, and they buy you an extra hour outdoors.

Assumptions worth confirming

  • 8–9 JanuaryThe bookings end on the 8th and you fly on the 9th, so day 16 is planned as a reposition to Montréal with a night near YUL. Flying out of Québec City instead? Keep that day here and ignore the train.
  • Angus → Montréal, 2 JanPlanned as a one-way drive with a lunch stop in Kingston. Roughly 650 km, 6h30 clean, 8–9 hours realistically with nine people and a baby.
  • Hours changeEvery time and price here is a planning estimate. Holiday-week hours are unpredictable — check each place the night before.
  • Weather rewrites daysWhich is why every day has three or more options and at least one of them is indoors.
